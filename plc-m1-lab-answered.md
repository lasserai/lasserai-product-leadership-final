# Write a Strategy That Can Survive a Test, Module 1 Lab

## Write your one-page strategy

- **Winning aspiration: define winning in the customer's terms, not internal metrics. What does success look like for the specific person you serve?:**

  Own the shortest path from field observation to resolved enterprise action. Meridian wins when an important site observation becomes an accountable action and gets resolved, without turning the field user into an administrator. The phone captures what happened; Meridian makes sure something happens next.

- **Where to play: segment, geography, and use case, explicitly. Which customers, markets, and surfaces will you focus on?:**

  Focus on high-value field observations requiring follow-through:
  - Field issues and exceptions
  - Safety and quality observations
  - Inspection findings
  - Progress exceptions
  - Coordination issues

  **Primary user:** Superintendent / foreman  
  **Enterprise beneficiary:** Project management, compliance and enterprise functions.

  **Boundary:** Meridian does not need to replace every photo, message or conversation. It should own observations where something needs to happen next.

- **How to win: your differentiator. What can you do that your specific competitors cannot easily replicate?:**

  Make one valuable behavior habitual through this loop:

  **Observe → Capture → Understand → Route → Act → Resolve → Learn**

  - **Capture effortlessly:** Photo, voice or simple entry using natural field behavior.
  - **Turn observation into action:** Automatically add context, structure, routing and accountability.
  - **Close the loop:** Make ownership, progress and resolution visible without duplicate reporting.
  - **Learn from outcomes:** Use completed workflows, user corrections and outcomes as feedback to improve workflows and AI assistance.

  The intended habit is: “When I see something that needs action, I capture it once in Meridian.”

  Meridian's differentiator is **field simplicity + enterprise depth + continuous learning**. Field users get less reporting, less chasing and visible follow-up. The enterprise gets structured information, accountability and a trusted project record. Meridian gains a feedback channel from real observation → action → outcome loops to evaluate and improve future workflows and AI-assisted features.

- **Capabilities required: what you must be world-class at. What will you build, buy, or partner for?:**

  Based on the Word document, required capabilities include:
  - Effortless field capture through photo, voice or simple entry.
  - Adding context and structure, and routing observations with clear accountability.
  - Making ownership, progress and resolution visible without duplicate reporting.
  - Using completed workflows, user corrections and outcomes to improve workflows and AI assistance.

  The document does not specify which capabilities should be built, bought or obtained through partners.

- **Management systems: the metrics and rituals that keep your choices alive quarter to quarter.:**

  **Number One metric:** Percentage of actionable field observations managed end-to-end through Meridian. This defines winning—not logins, uploads or feature usage.

  Diagnose the Number One through:
  1. **Adoption:** Do target users try the core workflow?
  2. **Repeat:** Do they choose Meridian again for the next relevant observation?
  3. **Habit:** Does Meridian become the normal path for qualifying observations?
  4. **Value:** Does Meridian reduce time, effort or lost follow-up?
  5. **Learning:** Do completed workflows provide useful feedback for improving the next experience?

  **Negative metric to watch:** One-and-done rate—the percentage of first-time users who complete a meaningful workflow but do not return for another relevant workflow.

  The Word document does not specify recurring management rituals.

## Name your one hard no

- **Your one hard no:**

  No investment that does not strengthen the **observation → action → resolution → learning** loop. New AI, analytics, reporting or enterprise capabilities are valid when they make the loop materially better.

## Write your three OKRs

- **Objective:**

  Make Meridian the habitual path from significant field observation to accountable action.

- **KR1: Workflow adoption**

  Increase the share of qualifying observations managed end-to-end through Meridian from X% to Y%.

- **KR2: Repeat behavior**

  Increase the proportion of users who return to Meridian for their next qualifying field workflow from X% to Y%.

- **KR3: Value**

  Reduce median time from observation to accountable action from X to Y.

**Additional KR included in the Word document — Efficiency:** Reduce manual and duplicate field reporting effort by X%.

The document recommends keeping AI/learning as a strategic flywheel and product-health measure, rather than creating a KR for “training AI,” which could encourage optimizing data volume instead of customer value.

## AI pressure-test

- **Which challenge from the AI is most valid, and why?:**

  The Word document does not explicitly name an AI challenge. Its clearest strategic concern is that adoption should not be treated as the end goal: acquisition or mandated use may look successful while the product fails to create a habit. It also cautions that a “training AI” KR could lead the organization to optimize data volume instead of customer value.

- **What would you change based on the pushback, and what would you defend?:**

  **Change:** Make habit, realized value and the learning flywheel explicit parts of the strategy rather than treating adoption as the end goal.

  **Defend:** Keep the focus on high-value field observations that need follow-through, and make the end-to-end observation-to-resolution loop effortless. The strategic thesis is: “Capture creates action. Action creates outcomes. Outcomes create learning. Learning makes the next action easier.”
