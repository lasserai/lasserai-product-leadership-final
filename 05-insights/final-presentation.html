Meridian case

Meridian will win with large North American general contractors by making mobile field reporting dramatically faster and more reliable, betting that reducing friction for foremen and superintendents will drive field adoption and improve project outcomes.

The case

Module 1
Product strategy
Where you play and how you win

Winning aspiration: Make Meridian the field team's fastest, easiest way to capture progress, resolve issues, and keep work moving, so superintendents and foremen spend less time on admin and more time building.
Where to play: Focus on enterprise construction projects in North America, starting with large general contractors whose field teams rely on phone cameras and group texts. Prioritize mobile workflows for daily progress updates, photo documentation, and issue tracking.
How to win: Deliver a mobile-first, field-friendly experience that turns photos, voice notes, and quick updates into structured project records automatically. Unlike generic messaging apps, Meridian connects field activity directly to schedules, budgets, and compliance records without requiring duplicate data entry.

The hard no

We will not build a separate, fully customizable field app that replicates every enterprise feature. Instead, we will prioritize a simple, mobile-first experience for essential field tasks and connect it to Meridian’s existing enterprise platform. This protects ease of use and adoption among field teams while preserving enterprise depth without creating a second, overly complex product.

Objective and key results

Make Meridian indispensable to field teams by making daily project updates faster and easier while preserving enterprise visibility.
Increase the share of qualifying observations Increase weekly active field-user adoption from 30% to 70% of eligible field teams.
Reduce the average time field teams spend submitting daily progress updates from 15 minutes to 5 minutes per day.
Increase the percentage of field issues logged in Meridian and resolved with a documented record from 40% to 80%.

Module 2
Outcome roadmap
Now, next, and later

Now: We bet simplifying daily reporting, enabling offline use, and designing a field-first experience will increase weekly active field-user adoption from 30% to 70%, reduce reporting time from 15 to 5 minutes, and improve documented issue resolution from 40% to 80%.
Next: We bet improving photo-based issue capture, schedule notifications, and data integration will help field teams document problems faster, respond to changes sooner, and resolve issues more efficiently.
Later: We bet automating field documentation and compliance workflows with AI will reduce administrative effort and improve project data quality for field teams and enterprise project managers, once core field adoption is established.

Module 3
Team charter
What we own

What this team owns; We own the field-adoption strategy and roadmap, including simplifying daily logs from 14 required fields to 4, reliable offline mobile workflows, and a field-first experience. We measure success by increasing weekly active field-user adoption from 30% to 70%, reducing reporting time from 15 to 5 minutes, and increasing documented field-issue resolution from 40% to 80%.
Out of scope; We will not add enterprise features, standalone modules, or cosmetic redesigns to the Foundations roadmap solely because Sales requests them. We will not commit engineering capacity to renewal-related features without an explicit resource-allocation decision. We will not duplicate the full enterprise platform in the field app.
Joint calls across boundaries; PM 1 (field adoption) and PM 2 (enterprise) must agree before changing shared data models, integrations, security, or engineering allocations that affect both roadmaps. Any sales commitment that changes Foundations scope or delivery dates also requires agreement from the affected PM and engineering lead before it is promised to a customer.

How we decide

Feature and scope calls; PM 1 decides field-workflow priorities within the agreed strategy and capacity. PM 2 decides enterprise feature priorities within the enterprise roadmap and capacity. The engineering lead decides technical implementation and raises feasibility or reliability concerns. The Foundations product lead decides trade-offs affecting Foundations scope, OKRs, or capacity, after consulting the affected PMs and engineering lead.
Cross-team conflicts; PM 1 and PM 2 first attempt to resolve the conflict directly within 1 business day, using customer impact, engineering effort, renewal risk, and OKR impact. If unresolved, they escalate to the Foundations product lead, who makes or proposes a decision within 2 business days. No engineering capacity is redirected while the conflict is unresolved unless an authorized leader approves an exception.
Escalations from outside the team; The shared product executive resolves conflicts that exceed the Foundations product lead's authority or require a trade-off between enterprise renewal commitments and field-adoption goals. The enterprise sales leader provides evidence of renewal risk but cannot independently reassign engineering capacity. The decision and its impact on both roadmaps must be documented and communicated within 3 business days of escalation.

Module 5
Financial model
The business case

Strategic Rock: Simplify daily field reporting from 14 required fields to 4. 1. The strategic bet We bet that reducing daily reporting from 14 required fields to 4 will make Meridian easier for field foremen and superintendents to use, increasing adoption and improving the completeness of project records. Better field data should help project teams identify and resolve issues sooner, creating potential retention and Enterprise expansion benefits. We will fund a 90-day controlled pilot before committing to a full rollout. The pilot must demonstrate measurable user benefits and provide evidence of a credible financial return. 2. The assumptions — ranked by risk Rank Assumption How we will test it 1 — Critical Reporting friction is a major cause of low field adoption. Compare adoption for pilot teams with comparable control teams. 2 Reducing required fields from 14 to 4 cuts reporting time by at least 30%. Measure reporting time before and during the pilot. 3 Increased adoption improves issue documentation and customer outcomes. Track documented issue resolution and time to resolution. 4 Better field outcomes improve retention or Enterprise expansion enough to justify investment. Compare renewal signals and expansion opportunities, then validate with account-level financial data. The largest risk is that simpler forms save time but do not change adoption or customer economics. 3. Expected return Operational value Required fields 14 → 4 71% fewer required fields Reporting time target 15 → 5 min 10 minutes saved per report Adoption target 30% → 70% Weekly active eligible field users Issue documentation target 40% → 80% Issues resolved with a documented record Assuming five reports per week over 50 working weeks, saving 10 minutes per report creates approximately 43 hours saved per regular field user per year. Across 100 regular users, that is about 4,167 hours annually. Financial return The financial benefit will be calculated from verified customer outcomes, not assumed in advance: - Retention benefit: eligible revenue retained due to a measured reduction in churn. - Expansion benefit: incremental gross profit from attributable Enterprise upgrades. - Net return: incremental gross profit minus build, rollout, and ongoing operating costs. - Payback: initial investment divided by verified incremental monthly gross profit. We do not yet have the build cost, account-level contract values, gross margins, or evidence of churn and expansion lift. Therefore, a credible dollar ROI or payback period cannot yet be claimed. These inputs must be established before full-rollout funding. 4. The kill criterion At the end of the 90-day pilot, compare results against a comparable control group. Stop the rollout and reallocate the uncommitted engineering budget if either: - Weekly active field-user adoption improves by less than 10 percentage points versus the control group; or - Average reporting time falls by less than 30% versus baseline. Even if these operational thresholds are met, do not approve full rollout unless the updated financial model shows a credible path to positive incremental return and an acceptable payback period under a downside scenario. The Foundations product lead owns the decision, with Finance validating the economics before further funding. Funding verdict FUND WITH ONE CONDITION: approve the 90-day pilot only, with full rollout conditional on meeting the adoption and reporting-time thresholds and demonstrating a financially credible return. This limits the initial commitment, tests the most important assumption with real field data, and prevents operational improvements from being mistaken for proven financial value.

Kill criterion

Stop further rollout funding if, after a 90-day pilot, weekly active field-user adoption has not improved by at least 10 percentage points versus a comparable control group, or reporting time has not fallen by at least 30%. The consequence: cancel the next rollout phase and reallocate the uncommitted engineering budget to the highest-performing Foundations priority before committing additional capacity.

Module 6

What I learned

Where the strategy was hardest to write, what you will keep doing, and the moment your thinking changed.

Friction

The most challenging topic is usually dealing with people that may not same interests with me but are needed to accomplish some goal. Usually one-on-one honest discussion to understand where the common ground helps out moving forward.

Key learnings

Strategic bets, hard nos, shortest tests, killing criteria I've been already started using and practicing with my teams.

Aha moment

The hard nos and also killing criteria besides success criteria beforehand helps out to avoid unnecessary pondering when executing.
