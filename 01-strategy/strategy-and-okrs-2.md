## 1. Playing to Win cascade

| Question | Your choice |
|---|---|
| **Winning aspiration** | Make Meridian the field team's fastest, easiest way to capture progress, resolve issues, and keep work moving, so superintendents and foremen spend less time on admin and more time building. |
| **Where to play** | Focus on enterprise construction projects in North America, starting with large general contractors whose field teams rely on phone cameras and group texts. Prioritize mobile workflows for daily progress updates, photo documentation, and issue tracking. |
| **How to win** | Deliver a mobile-first, field-friendly experience that turns photos, voice notes, and quick updates into structured project records automatically. Unlike generic messaging apps, Meridian connects field activity directly to schedules, budgets, and compliance records without requiring duplicate data entry. |
| **Capabilities required** | Build: Simple offline-capable mobile workflows, fast photo capture, voice-to-report tools, and automatic links to project records. Build: Seamless integration between field updates and Meridian's existing enterprise planning and reporting tools. Partner or buy: Speech-to-text and AI capabilities for converting field notes into structured updates. Excel at: Field-user research, mobile usability, and reliable performance on busy construction sites. |
| **Management systems** | Track: Weekly active field users, repeat usage, time to submit updates, and percentage of updates captured directly in Meridian. Measure outcomes: Reduced reporting time, faster issue resolution, and improved accuracy of project records. Rituals: Review field-user feedback and adoption weekly; conduct monthly site visits; review adoption, customer outcomes, and enterprise impact quarterly. Decision rule: Prioritize changes that reduce field friction and increase repeat usage without compromising enterprise reporting, security, or project controls. |

## 2. Your one hard no

> **Hard no:** We will not build a separate, fully customizable field app that replicates every enterprise feature. Instead, we will prioritize a simple, mobile-first experience for essential field tasks and connect it to Meridian’s existing enterprise platform. This protects ease of use and adoption among field teams while preserving enterprise depth without creating a second, overly complex product.

## 3. OKR cascade

> **Objective:** Make Meridian indispensable to field teams by making daily project updates faster and easier while preserving enterprise visibility.
>
> - **KR1:** Increase the share of qualifying observations Increase weekly active field-user adoption from 30% to 70% of eligible field teams.
> - **KR2:** Reduce the average time field teams spend submitting daily progress updates from 15 minutes to 5 minutes per day.
> - **KR3:** Increase the percentage of field issues logged in Meridian and resolved with a documented record from 40% to 80%.

## 4. AI pressure-test notes

| Prompt question | Your answer |
|---|---|
| Which challenge from the AI is most valid, and why? | The most valid challenge is the assumption that poor usability is the main reason field teams don't adopt Meridian. Field workers already use phone cameras and group texts because these tools are familiar, fast, and easy to use with subcontractors. Even if Meridian improves its mobile interface, workers may still resist if it adds extra steps or requires everyone on site to change their habits. This matters because solving the wrong problem could lead to a better product that people still don't use. Before investing in development, Meridian should observe field teams in real working conditions and test whether reducing friction and automatically turning photos and messages into project records would make a meaningful difference. |
| What would you change, and what would you defend? | What I would change: I would validate why field teams avoid Meridian before committing to a full mobile product redesign. I would run pilot tests on construction sites to determine whether the main barriers are usability, collaboration with subcontractors, or extra data entry. I would also strengthen the OKRs by measuring whether Meridian reduces issue-resolution time and rework, not just whether more people use the platform. What I would defend: I would defend the mobile-first, field-friendly strategy and the decision to connect field updates directly to Meridian’s enterprise platform. This addresses the needs of both field workers and project managers without sacrificing the enterprise capabilities customers value. I would also defend keeping the experience simple rather than replicating every enterprise feature in a separate app. |
